**Top 5 recurring complaints (what customers keep grumbling about)**  

| # | Complaint | Frequency cue in the reviews | Why it hurts the shopper experience |
|---|-----------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Fit is wildly off‑scale** – items run too large, are overly boxy/short, or flare in the wrong places (shoulder‑fat sleeves, “mushroom” tops, huge‑but‑short coats). | Mentioned in ≥ 12 reviews. | Customers can’t wear the piece, have to return it, and lose confidence in the brand’s size chart. |
| 2️⃣ **Fabric feels cheap or wrong** – chemical/“plastic” smell, excessive polyester, stiff or too‑stretchy material, rapid pilling, thin/see‑through texture. | Appears in ≥ 10 reviews. | The garment looks nice on the model but feels cheap in‑hand, leading to disappointment and a perception of low value for the price. |
| 3️⃣ **Construction defects** – holes after one wear, torn tags, loose threads, puffy‑paint embellishments that flake off, faulty zippers, missing elastic or hooks, uneven seams. | Cited in ≥ 9 reviews. | These flaws make the item look unfinished, cause wear‑and‑tear instantly, and raise doubts about quality‑control. |
| 4️⃣ **Mis‑leading presentation / “looks better on the hanger”** – colour/fit different from the website photo, overly drapey on thin bodies but “fat‑making” on larger chests, washed‑out hues. | Noted in ≥ 8 reviews. | Shoppers feel duped, leading to returns and lower trust in the brand’s visuals. |
| 5️⃣ **Lack of functional details** – no waist elastic/tringles on leggings, sleeves not lined, missing interior hooks/buttons, pockets that bulge, missing adjustability on straps. | Reported in ≥ 6 reviews. | The missing details make otherwise‑lovable designs unusable for daily wear. |

---

**Top 3 feature requests (what customers keep asking for)**  

| # | Requested feature | Rationale behind the request |
|---|-------------------|------------------------------|
| 1️⃣ **Higher‑quality, natural‑fiber fabrics** – more cotton, linen, silk, or heavier blends; less cheap polyester/nylon; fabrics that don’t smell, pill or feel “plastic”. | Customers love the design but abandon it when the material feels cheap; they’re willing to pay a premium for a better hand‑feel and durability. |
| 2️⃣ **More accurate, inclusive sizing & fit options** – a tighter/true‑to‑size cut, petite/plus‑size specifics, adjustable elements (e.g., removable straps, side seams), and clearer measurement guides. | Repeated “huge”, “boxy”, and “short” complaints indicate the current size chart doesn’t map to real bodies; shoppers want a fit that flatters their shape without drastic alterations. |
| 3️⃣ **Improved construction & functional details** – reinforced seams and tags, reliable zippers, interior hooks or buttons to keep coat edges aligned, waistbands with elastic or drawstrings, and properly dried/secure embellishments. | Faulty hardware and loose stitching cause premature damage; adding these small but vital touches would boost perceived quality and reduce returns. |

**Take‑away for the product team:**  
- **Audit the size chart and cut patterns** (especially sleeves, shoulders, and length) to reduce the “huge/boxy” syndrome.  
- **Shift material sourcing** toward natural or higher‑grade blends and eliminate the strong chemical odor.  
- **Tighten QC on seams, tags, zippers, and decorative paints**; add missing functional hardware (elastic waistbands, interior hooks) where the design calls for it.  

Addressing these three high‑impact areas should cut the most common complaints and satisfy the top‑ranked feature requests.
